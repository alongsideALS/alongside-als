Questions about ALS grants and financial assistance

Are there grants for people with ALS?

Yes. More than 100 grants and scholarships are listed above, about half of them nationwide and the rest for particular states or regions. Nationwide examples include the ALS Association's Hoffman ALS Financial Assistance Program (help getting to clinic visits), the HealthWell Foundation ALS Fund (prescription copays and Medicare Part B premiums) and the MDA equipment grant. Most local ALS Association chapters and ALS United organizations also run their own care grants.

What can ALS grants pay for?

The most common uses are respite care and caregiving, equipment and communication technology, home changes such as ramps and roll-in showers, travel to clinic, medical and prescription costs, help for children and families, and scholarships. Each listing says what it covers, how much it gives and when it is open.

Do ALS grants have to be paid back?

No. Grants and scholarships are not loans. Some programs pay the vendor or provider directly rather than giving cash, and "up to" amounts are maximums, not promised payments. Equipment and van loans are listed separately under Services.

What other financial help is there for ALS?

Government benefits often matter more than any single grant: Social Security Disability (ALS claims are fast-tracked and the waiting period is waived), Medicare that starts with disability benefits, and VA compensation for veterans. See benefits, loans and free help, and Get ready for the documents most applications ask for.
