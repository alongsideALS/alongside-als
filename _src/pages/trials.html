Understand the stages

What the phases mean

Phase 1 studies test safety in a small group, often with increasing doses. Phase 2 looks for signs of benefit and the right dose. Phase 3 compares the treatment with placebo in larger numbers to confirm benefit; these are the studies that lead to approval. Expanded access programs provide an investigational drug outside a trial to people who can't join one. Observational studies and registries collect information and samples without testing a treatment, and some can be done from home.

Platform trials like the HEALEY ALS Platform Trial test several drugs at once with a shared placebo group, so more participants get an active drug. Enrollment opens and closes by regimen.

Before you join

Questions to ask the study team

  • What is being tested, and what's the chance I get a placebo?
  • Is there an open-label extension where everyone gets the drug?
  • How many visits, how far, and are travel and lodging reimbursed?
  • Can I keep taking riluzole, edaravone or other medications?
  • Will joining this study keep me out of other trials?
  • What happens to my care when the study ends?

Questions about ALS clinical trials

How do I find an ALS clinical trial near me?

Choose your state in the filter above to see studies with a site there, or open your state's page from the map. Some observational studies can be done from home. Your ALS clinic and the ALS TDI and NEALS trial navigators listed above can help narrow the list.

Who can join an ALS clinical trial?

Each study sets its own criteria, often including how long ago symptoms started, breathing strength (vital capacity), age and sometimes a specific gene. The study team decides eligibility; the plain-language page for each study lists the basics and links to the full criteria on ClinicalTrials.gov.

Do ALS clinical trials cost money to join?

Study treatments and study visits are usually provided at no cost, and many studies help with travel. Ask the study team what is covered before you enroll.

Are there trials for genetic ALS, such as SOD1 or C9orf72?

Yes. Use the gene filter above to see studies for SOD1, C9orf72, FUS and other forms, including studies for family members who carry a gene. Genetic testing is now offered to everyone with ALS because the result can change trial options.
