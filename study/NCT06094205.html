BG-Speech-02

Leigh R. Hochberg, MD, PhD. · Device study · NCT06094205

Feasibility of the BrainGate2 Neural Interface System in Persons With Tetraplegia (BG-Speech-02)

In plain words
A UC Davis BrainGate study aiming to let people who cannot speak fluently communicate through a computer by attempting to speak. Involves brain surgery to place sensors and ongoing research sessions. No placebo; about 2 participants.
What it studies
An interventional study investigating a health-related research question. Listed approaches include a device.
Treatment being tested
Implanted BrainGate2 sensors in speech-related cortex; attempted speech decoded to a computer
Key eligibility
  • Ages 18-80 with ALS or another motor neuron disorder, spinal cord injury, brainstem stroke or muscular dystrophy
  • Complete or incomplete tetraplegia
  • Lives within a three-hour drive of UC Davis (Sacramento) and geographically stable for 15 months
  • Excludes chronic steroids/immunosuppressants, visual impairment, other serious disease

Highlights only; the full criteria are on ClinicalTrials.gov.

Who it is for
Listed research conditions: Anarthria; Dysarthria; Tetraplegia (and other listed conditions). Age range: 18 years to 80 years. Healthy volunteers are not listed as eligible. Other criteria apply; the study team determines eligibility.
Taking part
Treatment assignment is not blinded. Visit frequency, travel, procedures, and time commitment are not fully summarized here; confirm them with the study team. The registry lists a target of about 2 participants.
Timeline
Started October 2023; main results expected around August 2027. Registry estimates; dates often move.
US study sites

California: University of California, Davis (Sacramento)
